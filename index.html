Two things per session, and that is all you need. Slides are the deck shown in class, with the short animations playing inside it — arrow keys to move, F for full screen. Drills are the paper exercises we do together, in exam format, with the worked solutions folded at the bottom of the page.

You will not write any code in this course. The exam is on paper. The interactive demonstrations I drive from the projector are Colab notebooks and you are welcome to open one and move the sliders yourself, but nothing here asks you to, and nothing is marked on it. They live in the course repository, one per week.

How the five weeks work the contract, stated once

  • Every technique serves an exam item. Weeks 1–3 build the study of a function by hand for the midterm and Q5 of the final. Weeks 4–5 build the financial mathematics the exam and the resit ask for. You are told, each time, which one.
  • Decision → picture → technique → decision. Each block opens with a business question, shows the picture on screen, then does the technique on paper, and closes with one sentence you could say to a Board.
  • Half slides, half laboratory. About forty minutes of slides and notebook, eighty minutes of guided hand computation, a Board pitch by one student, ten minutes of homework briefing. Real break mid-session.
  • Weekly rhythm. Thursday lesson. Drill solutions and homework online by Friday. Homework comes back with you the following Thursday — on paper, at the start of class. Those first fifteen minutes are the walkthrough: one of you explains one drill at the board. Send it ahead by email if you would rather I read it sooner.
  • Homework is formative. It is not graded, but it is read, discussed, and it is part of the evidence used when the midterm is marked.
  • Running case: Arno Bikes, an e-bike assembler in Florence. Fixed cost €60,000 a month, variable cost €400 a bike, list price €1,000, demand p = 1600 − 4q. Every drill reuses its shape with different numbers, so that by week 3 the midterm case is a familiar object.

Assessment module scheme, set by ESE

  • Midterm assignment — 25%. A 1,500-word research paper (±10%) plus an 8–15 minute oral presentation. Written part 80%, oral 20%. Guidelines are briefed in class in week 2 and published on the student portal. Paper due Sunday 1 November, 23:59 on Turnitin; orals in the week of 2 November, on the day set by the module leader. No late submission for postgraduate work; a failed or missing submission gives one in-term resubmission capped at 50%.
  • Final examination — 75%. Three hours in class, five questions of equal weight, all work shown, non-programmable calculator only, no notes. Weeks 1–5 train you for the pure calculus question (limits, derivatives, integrals of rational functions with log terms) and the financial-mathematics items.
  • Reassessment. Re-sit examination, 100%, capped at 40% by ESE regulation.
