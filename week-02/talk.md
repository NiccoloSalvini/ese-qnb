### 5. First, the midterm (35')

"Before any mathematics, the midterm — while you are fresh, and because questions are cheap now and

Open the guidelines on screen and read them together. In order:

1. **What:** a 1,500-word paper (±10%) plus an oral of up to 15 minutes. 25% of the module; paper 80%,
   oral 20%. Due Sunday 1 November, 23:59, Turnitin. Oral in week 6.
2. **The function-study checklist** — write it on the board and leave it: domain → limits → range →

### 7. Block A — section slide (10")

### 8. A function is a machine (2')

"Something goes in, something comes out, and what happens inside is the function." Point at the
machine. **"One rule only: each input gives exactly one output. Put in 50 bikes, you get one cost —
never two."** "A spreadsheet cell, a pricing rule, a line of code: same picture."

### 9. This is my half of the course, in one sentence (1')

**"Take a function a firm lives by, and study it until you can say what it does."** That is the
midterm (study cost, revenue, profit, and sketch them) and it is one question of the final (pure
calculus, by hand). "Today the first pieces; next week the whole procedure."

### 10. Four functions, one firm (5')

Opening question: "Which of these is Arno Bikes' cost, and how do you know without being told?" Let
them argue: a constant plus something per bike → the line.

"Each one is a sentence about a business, written so it can be computed." Then the right column:
**"Domain is the first question, always: for which q does this mean anything?"** Read the three
restrictions; flag that the log is strict.

**Bet:** "π(100) and π(200). One is bigger — which, and by how much?" Get a number from each. Then:
both are €20,000. Silence. "The parabola is symmetric about 150: 100 and 200 are both 50 away."
Don't explain symmetry before the bet.

### 11. Nothing here is memorised (3')

"You never have to memorise a domain rule. Ask what the symbol is asking for." Go row by row:
the root asks *what number times itself gives x* — impossible if x is negative; the log asks
*to what power must e be raised* — a positive base never reaches zero or below; the fraction asks
*how many x fit into a* — nothing fits into nothing.

[click] "So in √(q−20) it is the thing *inside* that must be at least zero: q − 20 ≥ 0."
**"√0 is fine, ln 0 is not: that is the difference between the square bracket and the round one."**

### 13–14. Hub → Block B (20")

### 15. What does one bike cost? (2')

"The rent is €60,000 whether you build one bike or a thousand. Split it between the bikes." Point at
the two braces: the rent shared, the parts per bike.

**Bet:** "At 30 bikes, what does one bike cost the firm? At 300?" Collect. Answers: €2,400 and €600.

### 16. Clip — the rent, split thinner (1')

Play once, no commentary. Then: "What did you see?" Wait. **"The gold bar shrinks; the €400 floor
never moves."**

### 17. Where do you take a limit? (2')

"Inside the domain there is nothing to ask: you substitute and read. A limit is for the **edges** —
the places you cannot reach." Three rows: an excluded point (both sides), an open end (allowed side
only), a domain that runs on (infinity). **"Recipe: write the domain, list its edges, one limit per edge."**

### 18. Two limits, and a floor (3')

"AC has domain zero to infinity: two edges, two limits." Near zero: "one bike carries the whole rent
— ruinous." Far out: "the rent is spread so thin it vanishes, and average cost settles on €400. Never
below: every bike really needs €400 of parts." **"That is economies of scale, in two limits. A limit
is not the value at a point, it is where the function is heading."**

### 19. The one that looks impossible (4')

"Why at 10? Because 10 is outside the domain — an edge. Substitute and you get 0/0, which says
nothing." [click] Factor on the board: (q−10)(q+10) over (q−10), cancel, substitute: 20.

**Board sentence: "0/0 is not an answer, it is an instruction: factor, cancel, then substitute."**

### 23. How much does one more bike change profit? (2')

"You made 80 bikes last month. What does the eighty-first add?" **Bet:** more than €1,000, between 0

### 24. Clip — two points become one (1')

the slope at a point."

### 25. The definition, once (3')

Write the definition on the board. **"You will use this definition about twice in your life. What you
use every day is what it produces"** — the table: power, constant, log, exponential, sums term by term.
"That table is all the final needs." Point at the new row: "a fraction with q downstairs is just a power with a minus sign — we'll see it in a minute."

### 26. Differentiating, as a procedure (6')

"Never ask 'what is the derivative of this?'. Ask the questions in order: is it a sum? a number?
a fraction? a power? a log?" [click] Label the three pieces of π: power, power, number. Result −8q + 1200.
"A lone q is q to the one, so you keep just the number in front."

[click] The fraction, on the board: **"q downstairs is a negative power upstairs. 1/q² is q to the
minus two. Now the power rule, unchanged: the −2 comes down in front, take one off the power, minus
three. −2/q³ — negative, because the exponent was negative."** Then AC = 60,000/q + 400 →
AC′ = −60,000/q²: "negative at every q — average cost always falls. Block B's economies of scale,
now as a sign." Common slip to name: taking one off −2 and writing −1 instead of −3.

### 27. Question 6: something inside something? (4')

"ln(q+1) is not ln q. There is a function inside a function — and the midterm's revenue has exactly
this shape." **"Outside first, inside untouched, then multiply by the inside's derivative."**
[click] Do ln(3q) and (2q+5)⁴ on the board. "ln(q+1) looked free because the inside's derivative is 1."

### 36. From the derivative back to the function (3')

"The exam asks this in both directions. Given f′, find f." Power up by one. [click] **"The c is not
decoration: every 2q³ − q² + c has the same derivative. One extra fact pins it down."** f(1) = 4 → c = 3.

### 37. And it means something (2')

"Marginal cost 0.8q + 3 gives cost 0.4q² + 3q + c — and c is C(0), what you pay having produced
nothing." **"The constant of integration is not a technicality. It is the rent."**

### 38. Running it backwards has a name (2')

"Every derivative rule read right to left." Point at the power row and the 1/q row.
**"Power up by one, divide by the new power. The one power that fails, q to the minus one, is
exactly the one that becomes a log. And checking is free: differentiate your answer."**

### 39. The exam's integral: split the fraction first (3')

"The final's calculus question opens with a fraction. There is no rule for a fraction — so make it
stop being one." [click] Divide each term by q, integrate each piece. Board sentence.

### 39b. The definite integral, and what it measures (4')

"Put two numbers on the integral sign: the value at the top minus the value at the bottom. The c
cancels, so leave it out." ∫₀²⁰ (0.8q + 3) dq = 220: "the marginal cost of the first twenty, added
up — what they cost on top of the rent." [click] ∫₁₀₀¹⁵⁰ π′ = π(150) − π(100) = €10,000: "going from
100 to 150 bikes adds ten thousand euros a month."
**"The integral of the marginal is the change in the total. That sentence is the whole of integration."**
Board sentence: **"Differentiate to ask what one more does. Integrate to ask what all of them did."**
