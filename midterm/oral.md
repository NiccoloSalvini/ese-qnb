# The midterm oral — how to prepare it

**Week of 2 November, slot as in the timetable. Up to 15 minutes, PowerPoint, a summary of your paper. The oral is 20% of the midterm.** Optional reading for the reading week — not homework.

The paper is read; the oral is heard. A Board member who skims your slides for ten seconds should know what you recommend. Everything below follows from that.

## How the oral is marked, and what each line means in practice

| criterion | weight | what it means when you build the slides |
|---|---|---|
| **quality of the work presented** | 30% | the numbers are right and the sketches are clean; every claim on a slide is one you can defend |
| **use of theory and course material** | 30% | the vocabulary of the course, used correctly: domain, limits, marginal, convex and concave, maximum confirmed by the second derivative, break-even |
| **value to the audience** | 30% | a recommendation a Board could act on, stated on the first slide and repeated on the last |
| **delivery** | 10% | you speak to the room, not to the screen; you finish on time |

Three of the four lines are about content. Polish helps, but a clear argument with honest numbers wins.

## Fifteen minutes, slide by slide

A plan that fits; adapt it, but keep the order of the argument.

| | slide | time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **title and the recommendation in one sentence** | 0:30 |
| 2 | the firm and the question the Board asked | 1:00 |
| 3 | the risks from outside — the two or three that matter most, each with its source and the term of the function it moves | 2:00 |
| 4 | cost and revenue — one sketch each, with the points that matter marked | 3:00 |
| 5 | profit — the sketch, the best output, the break-evens | 3:00 |
| 6 | the tariff — before and after, in one table and one sketch | 2:00 |
| 7 | protectionism — your argument and its evidence | 1:30 |
| 8 | **the recommendation again, with the two numbers that support it** | 1:00 |
| 9 | references — shown, not read | — |

That is about fourteen minutes, which leaves room for the question.

## Showing a function study aloud

Do not read the seven steps off the slide. Put the sketch up and **walk along the curve**:

> "Profit starts here, at minus the fixed cost — at zero output we still pay the rent. It rises, crosses zero here, at the first break-even. It peaks here: that is the best output, and it is a genuine maximum because the second derivative is negative everywhere. Then it falls and crosses zero again: past that point every extra unit loses money."

Every number you say gets its sentence for the Board, exactly as in the paper. Keep the full seven-step working in the paper or in a backup slide, ready if asked.

## The unscripted question

You will be asked at least one question you have not seen, almost always about your own sketch. You cannot memorise the answer; you can understand the curve well enough to reason in front of the examiner. Practise with questions of this kind:

- Why does the curve bend the way it does?
- What happens to the best output if the fixed cost doubles? *(Week 3, block B: think before you answer.)*
- If the tariff were twice as large, would the break-evens move closer or further apart?
- Of your sources, which do you trust most, and why?

"Let me look at the sketch" is a perfectly good first sentence. Silence is not.

## Slides that work

- **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs two titles, it is two slides.
- **Sketches large**, axes labelled, units on the axes, key points marked and named.
- **Rounded numbers on the slides** (450 units, €28,700); the exact ones in the paper.
- **No paragraphs.** What you would write as a paragraph, you say.
- Cite sources on the slide where you use them, short form; the full list on the last slide.

## Before the day

- [ ] Rehearsed aloud, at least twice, with a timer: under 15 minutes
- [ ] Recommendation on the first and the last slide
- [ ] Every sketch readable from the back of the room
- [ ] Able to explain, pointing at each sketch, why the curve turns where it does
- [ ] Sources on the slides match the paper's reference list
- [ ] File saved as PowerPoint and as PDF, in case the room's laptop disagrees with yours
